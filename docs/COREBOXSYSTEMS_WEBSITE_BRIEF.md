# Corebox Systems Website Brief

## Purpose

This brief defines the first public website direction for Corebox Systems based on the current logo and the previously established domain split plan.

The site should live at `www.coreboxsystems.com` and act as the parent-company website.

Atlas should remain a product under the Corebox Systems brand and should live at `atlas.coreboxsystems.com`.

## Brand Read from the Logo

The current logo suggests a strong visual language:

- geometric
- engineered
- dimensional
- modern
- technical without looking cold

The mark uses a structured cube-like form with strong white, navy, and electric blue contrast. It reads as:

- systems thinking
- precision
- architecture
- modular products
- connected infrastructure

That makes it a strong fit for a company that builds operational software across multiple industries.

## Brand Positioning

Corebox Systems should be presented as a systems company, not just a software shop.

The public message should emphasize:

- practical software for real-world operations
- product thinking grounded in workflow clarity
- software that supports institutions, teams, and service-based organizations

This is important because the company is not only about Atlas. The public site must establish a parent brand that can support:

- education products
- home automation products
- veteran benefits guidance tools

## Website Goal

The first version of the site should do four things clearly:

1. establish Corebox Systems as the parent company
2. introduce Atlas as the first live product
3. signal that additional products are coming
4. create a contact or demo path for conversations

## Audience

Primary audiences:

- school and tutoring operators evaluating Atlas
- business partners or clients evaluating Corebox Systems as a company
- early prospects for future products

Secondary audiences:

- referral partners
- investors or strategic contacts
- technical collaborators

## Recommended Visual Direction

The site should feel bold, clean, and engineered.

### Tone

- confident
- modern
- structured
- premium
- direct

### Avoid

- soft startup gradients with generic SaaS copy
- overly playful education-only visuals
- stock-tech cliches
- a layout that feels like a CRM login wrapper

### Color Direction

Use the logo as the source of truth.

Core palette direction:

- deep navy
- electric blue
- white
- steel gray

Suggested usage:

- dark or steel-toned foundation
- bright blue for emphasis and motion
- white for sharp contrast and key surfaces

### Typography Direction

Use typography that feels engineered and intentional.

Suggested direction:

- a bold display face for headlines
- a clean technical sans-serif for body and UI
- generous spacing and strong alignment

### Motion Direction

Motion should feel deliberate and architectural.

Suggested patterns:

- staggered reveal of product blocks
- subtle light sweeps or glow accents
- panel transitions that reinforce a modular systems aesthetic

## Core Message

Recommended brand statement:

Corebox Systems builds focused software for real-world operations.

Supporting message:

We design products that bring structure, visibility, and control to complex workflows across education, automation, and service-driven environments.

## Homepage Structure

## 1. Hero

Purpose:

- introduce Corebox Systems as the parent brand
- establish authority immediately

Recommended headline directions:

- Software built for systems that have to work
- Operational software for the people doing real work
- Systems-first products for modern operations

Recommended supporting copy:

Corebox Systems creates software that brings clarity to the moving parts behind education, automation, and service-focused operations.

Recommended CTA:

- Explore Products
- Book a Demo
- Talk to Us

## 2. Product Portfolio Section

Purpose:

- show that Corebox is a platform company with multiple product tracks

Recommended structure:

- Atlas
- Home automation product placeholder
- Veteran benefits product placeholder

Recommended framing:

- Atlas: live now
- Additional focused products: in development

## 3. Atlas Spotlight

Purpose:

- give the live product credibility
- let education prospects understand immediate value

Recommended copy direction:

Atlas helps tutoring and education teams manage scheduling, communication, and student progress in one operating system.

Possible CTA:

- Visit Atlas
- Request an Atlas Demo

## 4. Why Corebox Systems

Purpose:

- explain what makes the company different

Recommended pillars:

- built around workflows, not buzzwords
- designed for clarity and accountability
- structured for real teams and real operations

## 5. Future Product Vision

Purpose:

- show ambition without sounding unfinished

Recommended framing:

Corebox Systems is expanding into additional software products for connected environments, home systems, and guided benefits support.

This should feel intentional, not speculative.

## 6. Contact or Demo Section

Purpose:

- convert interest into conversation

Recommended CTA options:

- Schedule a conversation
- Request a demo
- Contact Corebox Systems

- Partners

- confident
- operational

It should not sound like:

- generic agency messaging
- education-only branding

### Hero headline

Software built for systems that have to work.

### Hero supporting line

### Product section intro

## Design Notes from the Logo

The logo carries depth and glow. The website should reflect that without becoming visually noisy.

Recommended visual motifs:

- subtle panel lighting
- grid or structural linework
- modular card layouts
- restrained glow behind key brand elements
- hard-edged section framing rather than soft rounded SaaS patterns

The logo should be treated as premium and dimensional. Avoid flattening the entire site into generic white cards on a blank background.

## Launch Recommendation

For version 1:

- launch a strong one-page site first if needed
- include the key sections from this brief
- keep the message company-first and Atlas-supported

Then expand into deeper pages once traffic, demos, and product breadth justify it.
