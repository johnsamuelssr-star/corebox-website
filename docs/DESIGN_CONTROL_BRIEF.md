# Corebox Systems Design Control Brief

## Purpose

This document is the visual control layer for the Corebox Systems public website.

It should be used alongside the existing strategic brief.

- The **strategic brief** defines positioning, message, and site purpose.
- The **design control brief** defines visual rules, hierarchy, and layout behavior.

This document exists to prevent the site from drifting into generic SaaS styling, weak hierarchy, or over-designed visual noise.

## Relationship to the Strategic Brief

The strategic brief remains valid.

This document does **not** replace it.

It locks down how the site should look and behave so that implementation stays consistent with the intended brand direction.

## Non-Negotiable Brand Truths

1. **Corebox Systems is the parent company.**
2. **Atlas is the only live product and must visually dominate all product-related content.**
3. **Future products are expansion areas, not equal product cards.**
4. The public site must feel like a **serious software company website**, not a repurposed application shell.
5. The design language should feel **engineered, structured, premium, and modern**.

## Site Experience Goal

The site should feel like a company that builds operational systems with discipline.

The impression should be:

- confident
- architectural
- polished
- product-led
- technically credible
- restrained rather than flashy

The site should not feel:

- playful
- bubbly
- startup-template generic
- overly soft
- hazy or glow-heavy
- dependent on empty marketing language

## Scope of This Brief

This design control brief applies to:

- the public homepage at `www.coreboxsystems.com`
- the top-level public navigation and footer
- product presentation on the marketing site

This brief does **not** define the Atlas app UI.

---

# 1. Visual Hierarchy Rules

## Primary Hierarchy

The homepage hierarchy must be:

1. **Corebox Systems as the parent brand**
2. **Atlas as the real, live proof of execution**
3. **Corebox operating philosophy / value**
4. **Expansion areas for future products**
5. **Contact / demo conversion path**

## Product Hierarchy

Atlas must receive:

- the most visual weight
- the most copy depth
- the most concrete proof
- the clearest CTA

Future product areas must receive:

- lower contrast
- less copy
- less visual weight
- no primary CTA that competes with Atlas

## Content Density Rule

The homepage should feel **edited**.

Every section should have one clear purpose.
No section should try to explain everything.
No paragraph in the hero should exceed two short sentences.

---

# 2. Layout System

## Grid

Use a structured desktop grid.

Recommended behavior:

- max content width: `1200px` to `1280px`
- core layout grid: `12 columns`
- generous outer gutters
- clear vertical spacing between sections

The grid should be visible in the discipline of alignment, even if grid lines are not visibly drawn.

## Section Width Behavior

Use three width modes only:

1. **Standard content width** for copy-led sections
2. **Wide product width** for hero and Atlas proof sections
3. **Narrow reading width** for contact copy and supporting statements

Avoid inconsistent widths from section to section.

## Section Spacing

Spacing should feel deliberate and premium.

Recommended desktop rhythm:

- top-level section padding: `96px` to `140px`
- tight internal spacing within a panel: `20px` to `32px`
- major spacing between headline and paragraph: `16px` to `24px`

Do not use cramped spacing.
Do not use oversized empty luxury spacing without visual structure.

---

# 3. Hero Layout Rules

## Hero Purpose

The hero must immediately establish:

- Corebox Systems is a real software company
- the brand is structured and premium
- Atlas is part of a larger product vision

## Hero Composition

The hero should use a **split layout**, not a centered startup layout.

### Required structure

- **Left side:** headline, short supporting copy, CTA group
- **Right side:** brand object and/or product frame with structured visual depth

### Left side rules

- headline should be dominant
- supporting copy should be short and direct
- CTA stack should appear quickly without scrolling
- no oversized paragraph block

### Right side rules

The right side may include one of the following:

- an architectural treatment of the logo mark
- a structured product frame that hints at Atlas
- a controlled composition that combines brand object + interface proof

The right side must not become:

- a collage of random floating cards
- a pile of tiny UI panels
- a decorative abstract illustration with no product credibility

## Hero Height

Recommended behavior:

- desktop: `80vh` to `95vh`
- mobile: content-led height, not forced full-screen if it hurts readability

## Hero Copy Rules

- headline maximum: 2 lines on desktop if possible
- supporting copy maximum: 2 short sentences
- CTA count: 1 primary, 1 secondary
- no tertiary CTA in the hero

## Hero Visual Tone

The hero should feel:

- dark
- dimensional
- sharply lit in controlled areas
- crisp rather than atmospheric

It should not feel foggy, soft, or cinematic in a vague way.

---

# 4. Section Rhythm

## Required Homepage Rhythm

The homepage should follow this sequence:

1. **Hero**
2. **Products / platform overview**
3. **Atlas proof block**
4. **Why Corebox Systems**
5. **Expansion areas**
6. **Contact / demo close**

## Rhythm Behavior

The page should alternate between:

- broad, high-impact sections
- tighter explanatory sections

This prevents the site from becoming visually monotonous.

## Contrast Between Sections

Use contrast through:

- background depth shifts
- panel framing
- layout width changes
- copy density changes

Do not rely only on color changes.

## Scroll Experience Rule

At least one section after the hero should feel more restrained and informational.
At least one later section should feel more visually framed and product-led.

The page should not read like six identical dark blocks stacked vertically.

---

# 5. Typography Behavior

## Typography Goal

Typography should feel engineered and intentional.

It should support clarity first, then style.

## Type System Rule

Use only:

- **1 display family** for headlines
- **1 sans-serif family** for body copy, UI, and support text

Do not introduce a third type personality.

## Headline Behavior

Headlines should be:

- bold
- compact
- high contrast
- slightly tight in tracking
- clean in shape, not playful

Recommended behavior:

- weight: `700` to `800`
- line height: `1.0` to `1.1`
- tracking: slightly tightened for large display text

## Body Copy Behavior

Body copy should be:

- neutral
- readable
- controlled
- no oversized line length

Recommended behavior:

- weight: `400` to `500`
- line height: `1.45` to `1.65`
- max line length: `60` to `75` characters

## Eyebrow / Label Behavior

Section labels should be:

- uppercase or small caps if used
- short
- widely spaced enough to feel intentional
- used sparingly

## Product Name Behavior

- “Atlas” should be treated as a product name with stronger typographic emphasis
- future product area names should remain visually secondary

## Typography Anti-Patterns

Avoid:

- overly airy luxury spacing
- playful rounded type
- thin gray body copy on dark backgrounds
- huge blocks of center-aligned text

---

# 6. Shape Language, Radius, and Surfaces

## Border Radius

The site should use **small to moderate radius only**.

Recommended rule:

- panels: `12px` to `18px`
- buttons: `10px` to `14px`
- large containers: consistent with panel radius family

## Radius Prohibition

Do not use:

- pill-shaped containers
- highly rounded bubbly cards
- mixed radius styles across sections

## Shape Language

The visual language should feel:

- framed
- modular
- architectural
- cut with intention

It should not feel soft or inflated.

## Surface Behavior

Use layered surfaces, not flat endless darkness.

Recommended surface stack:

- foundation background
- secondary section surface
- framed panel surface
- highlighted proof surface

Surfaces should be distinguishable through contrast, stroke, and subtle depth.

---

# 7. Panel Style

## Panel Rule

Panels should feel like part of a product system.

They should be:

- structured
- framed
- lightly elevated
- crisp at the edges

## Recommended Panel Treatment

- subtle border or stroke
- restrained shadow or ambient depth
- dark-to-mid tone surfaces
- occasional internal highlights

## Panel Prohibition

Avoid:

- floating glassmorphism panels everywhere
- soft blurry white cards
- overly transparent blocks
- neon outlines on every panel

## Panel Density

Do not fill the page with cards.

A few strong panels are better than many weak ones.

---

# 8. Color and Contrast Controls

## Color Role Assignment

Use color intentionally.

### Core roles

- **Near-black navy:** primary foundation
- **Deep navy / steel blue:** secondary surfaces
- **Electric blue / cobalt:** emphasis only
- **White:** key contrast, typography, logo framing, important panels
- **Cool gray:** structural support

## Contrast Rule

The site must remain highly legible.

Body text, CTA buttons, and product panels must pass a practical readability test on dark backgrounds.

## Blue Accent Rule

Electric blue should feel valuable because it is limited.

It should not be present at equal intensity across the whole page.

---

# 9. Glow and Lighting Limits

## Glow Philosophy

Glow is an accent, not a default atmosphere.

The logo supports subtle lighting effects, but glow must be controlled to preserve professionalism.

## Approved Glow Zones

Glow may appear in:

- the hero brand object
- one Atlas-focused proof area
- subtle interactive hover states

## Glow Prohibition

Do not use:

- page-wide blue haze
- continuous blurred background fog
- strong glow behind every card
- multiple glowing edges stacked together
- overbloomed effects that reduce sharpness

## Lighting Character

Light should feel:

- directional
- engineered
- deliberate
- restrained

Not dreamy. Not sci-fi fantasy. Not vaporwave.

---

# 10. Motion Rules

## Motion Goal

Motion should reinforce structure and precision.

It should not exist just to show that animation is possible.

## Approved Motion Types

- staggered fade-and-rise reveals
- subtle panel entrance transitions
- controlled light sweep on key surfaces
- button hover refinement
- micro-interactions on product emphasis areas

## Motion Speed

Motion should feel slightly slower than consumer-app motion.

Recommended behavior:

- smooth
- confident
- low-jitter
- minimal bounce

## Motion Prohibition

Avoid:

- constant drifting elements
- decorative parallax everywhere
- looping ambient movement with no purpose
- exaggerated spring motion
- frequent animation on scroll for every block

## Reduced Motion Rule

All animation should degrade cleanly for reduced-motion users.

---

# 11. Atlas Proof and Screenshot Treatment

## Atlas Role

Atlas is the proof that Corebox Systems is already shipping.

This section must feel concrete and credible.

## Atlas Presentation Rule

Atlas should be presented with:

- real interface screenshots
- clear product framing
- one concise problem/solution statement
- one direct CTA

## Required Visual Weight

Atlas should receive:

- the largest product panel on the page
- stronger contrast than future product references
- more spacing and breathing room
- a visibly more finished treatment

## Screenshot Rules

Use screenshots that show:

- actual workflow structure
- clean UI moments
- recognizable value

Avoid screenshots that are:

- too zoomed out to read
- cluttered with dense tables only
- visually random
- cropped in a way that hides product usefulness

## Screenshot Framing

Preferred treatment:

- one primary screenshot or composed product frame
- optional secondary crop details
- framed inside a controlled panel, not dropped loosely onto the page

## Atlas Copy Rule

Atlas copy should emphasize:

- who it is for
- what operational problem it solves
- that it is available now

It should not sound speculative.

## Atlas CTA

Use one clear CTA only, such as:

- Explore Atlas
- Request an Atlas Demo
- Visit Atlas

---

# 12. Future Products Framing

## Framing Rule

Future products must be framed as **expansion areas** or **product tracks**, not equal products.

Approved labels include:

- Expansion Areas
- Product Tracks
- Additional Focus Areas
- In Development

## Presentation Rule

Future product areas should appear as:

- lower-emphasis panels
- simple text blocks
- short descriptions
- no equal-size spotlight treatment

## Content Rule

Each future area should have:

- a name or category label
- one short descriptive line
- optional small supporting icon or marker

## What Not to Do

Do not present future products as:

- equal hero cards
- polished product launches
- fully developed offerings
- major CTA destinations

The site should communicate ambition without pretending breadth that does not yet exist.

---

# 13. Navigation and CTA Controls

## Header Behavior

The header should feel compact, premium, and stable.

Rules:

- logo should remain legible
- nav should be minimal
- CTA should be singular and intentional
- header should not feel crowded

## Navigation Scope for Version 1

Recommended nav items:

- Home
- Products
- Atlas
- About
- Contact

## CTA Hierarchy

Global CTA hierarchy:

1. Primary: contact/demo conversation
2. Secondary: Atlas exploration

Do not create a scattered set of competing buttons throughout the page.

## Button Style

Buttons should feel firm and modern.

Use:

- modest radius
- strong contrast
- clear label language
- subtle hover refinement

Do not use pill buttons or oversized shiny gradients.

---

# 14. Mobile Rules

## Mobile Goal

Mobile should preserve hierarchy, not merely stack desktop pieces.

## Mobile Hero Rule

On mobile:

- headline remains first
- supporting copy remains short
- CTA remains visible early
- visual object appears below or integrated without pushing all content below the fold excessively

## Mobile Screenshot Rule

Atlas screenshots must remain readable.

If necessary:

- use one tighter crop
- reduce decorative surrounding elements
- simplify layered composition

## Mobile Spacing Rule

Use tighter spacing than desktop, but do not collapse the layout into cramped blocks.

## Mobile Prohibitions

Avoid:

- glow-heavy hero on small screens
- tiny unreadable product screenshots
- stacked panels with identical styling and no hierarchy
- oversized nav treatment that competes with the brand

---

# 15. Visual Quality Bar

Before launch, the homepage should pass this test:

## The site should look like:

- a serious company with at least one real product
- a business that can grow into a portfolio
- a polished software brand with discipline

## The site should not look like:

- a freelance agency homepage
- a startup template with swapped colors
- a repurposed CRM landing page
- a login shell with marketing text added on top

---

# 16. Acceptance Checklist

A design direction is acceptable only if the answer to these questions is yes:

- Does the homepage clearly establish Corebox Systems as the parent brand?
- Are future products framed as expansion areas rather than equal offerings?
- Does the hero use strong split-layout composition rather than a generic centered SaaS pattern?
- Does the typography feel engineered, confident, and highly legible?
- Are border radius and panel styles consistent across the site?
- Are glow effects restrained to a few purposeful zones?
- Does motion reinforce structure rather than distract from it?
- Do Atlas screenshots feel real, useful, and product-led?
- Does the page rhythm avoid repeated identical blocks?
- Does the site feel premium without becoming flashy or vague?
- Would a visitor immediately understand that Corebox Systems is building software products, not just a single app shell?

If any of these answers are no, the design should be revised before implementation.
